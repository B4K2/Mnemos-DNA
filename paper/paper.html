Nucleotides at Two Timescales: Gated Delta-Rule Linear Attention with k-mer Fast-Weight Memory for DNA Sequence Modeling

A matched-budget ablation of KDA hybrids on the human genome, on a single 8 GB consumer GPU

Akshat

Preprint · checkpoints at ≈90% of the 5B-token budget · September 2026
Abstract Gated delta-rule linear attention (Kimi Delta Attention, KDA) has been validated on natural language but, to our knowledge, never on genomic sequence modeling, where the dominant long-context architectures are implicit convolutions (HyenaDNA) and selective state-space models (Caduceus). We pretrain a family of ~2.3M-parameter character-level KDA models on the human reference genome (GRCh38 [10]) at a 131,072 bp training context on a single RTX 5060 (8 GB), and ablate two additions one axis at a time: a per-k-mer fast-weight memory that attacks the finite-state recall bound of delta-rule recurrences (D06), and a single windowed local-attention layer plus a sequence-length curriculum (D07). Pure KDA shows no measurable long-range exact recall (copy advantage ≈ 0 beyond 64 bp); the k-mer fast-weight memory recovers strong recall out to 64 kbp (Δ-CE 0.62 nats at distance 65,536). Both arms hold language-model loss essentially flat from 8 K to 1 M bp of context and run 1 Mbp inference in 5.6 GB, whereas released HyenaDNA-1M and Caduceus checkpoints, evaluated through the same harness, OOM at 64 kbp on the same card. On fine-tuned downstream tasks the local window + curriculum arm transforms position-sensitive short-sequence tasks — Nucleotide Transformer splice-site accuracy rises from 0.618 to 0.922 — while degrading epigenetic-mark classification (GUE mean −5.3 points), evidence that a fixed ultra-long pretraining length tunes the recurrence's timescales away from sub-kilobase grammar and that the fix is a trade-off, not a free lunch. All comparisons are matched-tokenizer, matched-data, and labelled where pretraining budgets differ; we report negative and mixed results as found.

1Introduction

DNA foundation models face a context problem that language models do not: regulatory interactions span hundreds of kilobases, yet single-nucleotide resolution matters, so a sequence of interest is routinely 105–106 tokens long. The two families that dominate this regime — HyenaDNA's implicit long convolutions [3] and the Mamba-based Caduceus [4] — both replace quadratic attention with an O(N·log N) or O(N) mixer. A third family, gated delta-rule linear attention (Gated DeltaNet [2], Kimi Delta Attention [1]), has strong natural-language results but no published genomic evaluation. Its defining weakness is also its most interesting property for genomics: the recurrent state is a fixed d×d matrix, so exact recall of a distant motif is information-theoretically bounded, and prior work formalizes this recall–capacity trade-off [9]. Genomes, full of near-exact repeats whose differences are the signal, should be a stress test for exactly this bound.

This paper asks three questions on strictly consumer hardware (one RTX 5060 laptop GPU, 8 GB VRAM): (i) is KDA a viable base operator for a character-level DNA model at 131 kbp training context; (ii) where does its finite-state recall bound show up on genomic tasks, and can it be repaired within an O(N) budget; (iii) what does an ultra-long fixed pretraining context do to sub-kilobase downstream tasks. Contributions:

  • D06 — k-mer fast-weight memory. A KDA backbone in which every layer also owns a content-addressed memory: each 8-mer address stores a 16×16 fast-weight matrix updated by a gated delta rule. This converts the recall probe from a flat zero (pure KDA) to 0.62–0.87 nats of copy advantage at distances of 64 bp–64 kbp, while remaining O(N) and exact under chunked low-memory inference.
  • D07 — a short-grammar patch. D06 plus one windowed local-attention layer (w=256) and a 1 K→131 K sequence-length curriculum. It raises fine-tuned accuracy on 13 of 14 Genomic Benchmarks / Nucleotide Transformer tasks (splice sites +30.4 points) but costs a mean 5.3 points on the 31-task GUE suite — a documented trade-off between short-range grammar and the epigenetic-mark tasks the fixed-length parent was better at.
  • A same-harness efficiency comparison. Released HyenaDNA and Caduceus checkpoints are run through our identical evaluation harness (same hg38 windows, seeds, probes, bootstrap). Our arms sustain 1 Mbp single-sequence inference in 5.6 GB; the baselines OOM at 64 kbp (Caduceus-PS at 32 kbp) on the same card. Pretraining budgets differ and every such table says so.

Everything here is a matched-compute small-scale study in the spirit of mechanistic ablations, not a claim to beat HyenaDNA at its published scale. Models are ~2.3M parameters; checkpoints analysed are at ≈90% of the 5B-token schedule (§5).

2Background

Kimi Delta Attention. KDA [1] is a linear-attention recurrence with a channel-wise forget gate, per-head write strength β, ℓ2-normalized queries/keys, and a short causal convolution on q/k/v. The layer maintains a dk×dv state St updated by a gated delta rule — an outer-product write that first subtracts what the key currently retrieves, then writes the new value — giving it error-correcting (fast-weight-programmer [8, 9]) semantics at O(N) time and O(1) state per head.

The recall bound. Because St has fixed size, the number of (key, value) associations recoverable exactly is bounded; past that, writes interfere. In language this shows up on synthetic multi-query associative recall (MQAR) [9]; in DNA we operationalize it as a repeat-copy probe on real hg38 background (§6.2).

Baselines. HyenaDNA [3] pretrains character-level implicit-convolution models up to 1 M context on hg38; Caduceus [4] is a bidirectional, reverse-complement-equivariant Mamba MLM at 131 K context. We compare against their released checkpoints through our harness (§5), never re-implementations.

3Hypotheses

Following pre-registration practice, the following hypotheses were stated in the study plan before the D06/D07 comparison was run, so that outcomes cannot be reframed after the fact. Each is written as a falsifiable prediction; §6 reports the test for each, and Table 4 (§7) gives the verdicts.

  • H1 (efficiency; high prior confidence). Linear-complexity KDA arms will process strictly longer sequences than the convolutional and SSM baselines under a fixed 8 GB VRAM ceiling, with peak memory growing linearly in sequence length. Falsifier: any baseline matching or exceeding the KDA arms' maximum feasible context on the same card. Test: §6.3.
  • H2 (recall bound). Pure KDA's exact-recall accuracy will degrade past a distance threshold set by its finite d×d state capacity; the k-mer fast-weight memory, which factors exact 8-mer recall out of that state, will hold up at longer range; a windowed local layer will add exact recall only inside its ~256–511 bp span. Falsifier: pure KDA matching the memory-augmented arms at long range, or the memory arms degrading identically to pure KDA. Test: §6.2.
  • H3 (language-model loss; no prior stated). Pretraining perplexity across arms was pre-registered as genuinely uncertain, with no directional expectation. Test: §6.1.
  • H4 (downstream capability; no prior stated, dissociation expected). Downstream fine-tuned accuracy was pre-registered as uncertain, with the explicit caution that perplexity may not predict it. The D07-specific sub-hypothesis, formed after D06's splice-site failure and before D07 was trained: a local-attention window plus a length curriculum will repair sub-kilobase position-sensitive tasks that fixed-131 K pretraining damaged. Falsifier: D07 failing to close the gap to the random-init control on splice sites. Test: §6.5.

4Architecture

4.1 D06: KDA + per-k-mer fast-weight memory

The backbone is 8 layers, dmodel=128, 4 heads, vocabulary of 8 (A/C/G/T/N + specials), no positional encoding (NoPE), 2.32M parameters. Alongside each layer's KDA mixer sits a content-addressed memory keyed by the exact identity of the last k=8 nucleotides — 48=65,536 possible addresses. Each address stores not a vector but a small delta-rule fast-weight matrix (dk=dv=16): when position t's 8-mer address is a, the layer writes (kt, vt) projections of the hidden state into slot a's matrix with a learned write gate (bias init 0.0, i.e. σ=0.5), and reads slot a's matrix with the current key under a learned read gate (bias init −1.0, σ≈0.27), adding the result to the mixer output pre-o_proj. Two properties matter:

  • It targets the recall bound directly. Recall of "what followed this exact 8-mer last time" no longer competes for the KDA state's fixed capacity: the address space factors it out, and the delta rule inside each slot handles collisions of repeated 8-mers.
  • It stays O(N) and chunk-exact. The full table (≈0.6 GB per training sequence) is never materialized during the training forward — only chained per-chunk carries are computed — and chunked inference carries the table across chunks bitwise-exactly, which is what makes the 1 Mbp low-memory evaluation path legitimate (§6.3).

Two training details were load-bearing rather than incidental: gate parameters are kept in fp32 (scalar gates freeze in bf16), and the read/write gate initializations above came from a bias sweep after an early bug zeroed the write gate.

4.2 D07: one local-attention layer + a sequence-length curriculum

D07 is D06 with two additions, each motivated by a specific observed failure (§7): a random-init D06 fine-tuned with a locus-aware head reached 0.86 on NT splice-sites while the 4.5B-token 131 K-context checkpoint reached 0.82 — pretraining at a fixed 131 K length had tuned the recurrence's timescales away from 300–600 bp grammar.

  • Local window. Block 4's mixer becomes blocked causal local attention with window 256 (effective causal span 256–511 bp): exact token-level recall inside the window regardless of learned timescales. Window 256 rather than 512 was chosen so that a 300–600 bp task sequence is not resolvable in a single exact hop.
  • Length curriculum. Training length warms up 1 K→131 K over the first 10% of steps, then draws per-step lengths from a fixed mix {1 K: 0.25, 16 K: 0.25, 131 K: 0.5}. Tokens per step are unchanged (131,072), so the token budget and LR schedule are identical to D06. A loss-driven length controller was considered and rejected: bang-bang switching oscillates and is not reproducible across arms; the fixed mix keeps both regimes in the loss by construction, monitored by val_loss (131 K) and val_loss_short (1 K).

Micro-batching is capped by tokens in flight, not sequences (4×32,768 peaks at 6.17 GiB where 1×131,072 peaks at 4.35 GiB), which is what lets the curriculum train on the 8 GB card. D07 is 2.29M parameters.

5Experimental setup

Pretraining. Human reference genome GRCh38/hg38 [10], single-nucleotide tokenization (vocab 8), HyenaDNA's held-out-chromosome split, causal next-nucleotide objective. Budget 5B tokens (38,147 steps × 131,072 tokens), AdamW, lr 10−3 cosine to 0.1×, warmup 200 steps, bf16 with gradient checkpointing, batch 1×131,072 tokens/step. Hardware: one NVIDIA RTX 5060 Laptop GPU (8 GB, Blackwell). The checkpoints analysed are at step 34,177 (D06) and 33,900 (D07) — 89–90% of the schedule; training of the final 10% was still running when this draft was written, and all numbers below are from these ≈90% checkpoints.

Evaluation harness. Every number comes from one suite (scripts/benchmark.py): per-(benchmark, context) reseeding of numpy+torch, a 4M-token floor for LM evaluation per context, 128 trials per needle point, and a percentile bootstrap (n=1000) on every test-set metric, resampling per prediction (classification), per window (LM), or per trial (needle) — split-sampling uncertainty only, not seed variance. Downstream tasks run in two modes: a frozen-backbone linear probe (lower bound) and full fine-tuning (protocol family of HyenaDNA/NT/DNABERT-2; effective batch 32, up to 6 epochs / 6,000 steps, best-dev-epoch selection, a [mean | last | 8 positional bins] head pooled over forward and reverse-complement passes). External baselines — hyenadna-large-1m, hyenadna-medium-450k, caduceus-ph/ps 131k — run through the same harness with vocabulary remapped losslessly; their pretraining budgets are far larger than ours, and every table containing them is labelled accordingly. Caduceus is a bidirectional MLM, so causal LM/needle metrics are skipped for it by design. The chunked low-memory path (--offload) is used only for our KDA arms, where it is verified bitwise-exact; the baselines' OOM ceilings are real measurements, not artifacts of a missing offload path.

6Results

6.1 Pretraining (tests H3): matched loss, flat across three orders of magnitude of context

At the ≈90% checkpoints, held-out cross-entropy is 1.065 (D06) and 1.067 (D07) nats/base at 131 K context — 1.54 bits/bp, perplexity 2.90 over the 4-letter alphabet — and stays within ±0.03 nats from 8 K to 1 M bp of evaluation context for both arms. The local window and curriculum are loss-neutral at long range: whatever they change (§6.5), it is not next-nucleotide modeling of megabase windows.

6.2 The recall bound (tests H2) is real, and the k-mer memory removes most of it

This is the mechanistic centerpiece for H2 and it resolves cleanly. H2 predicted that pure KDA would degrade past a capacity threshold: in practice there is no threshold to find — D04's copy advantage is ≈0.01 nats at distance 64 and zero beyond, i.e. pure KDA never performs measurable exact recall at any distance on genomic background. The fast-weight memory (D06) recovers 0.87 nats at short range, still 0.62 at 64 kbp. D07 sits slightly below D06 at long distances (0.53 at 64 kbp) — its local window contributes exact recall only inside ~500 bp, and the curriculum's short-sequence steps plausibly de-emphasize ultra-long carry — but the two arms are qualitatively identical. An interesting negative within the positive: the decay from 0.85 to 0.53 nats between 4 K and 64 K is interference inside slot-level fast weights, not address exhaustion, since address collisions are distance-independent.

6.3 Efficiency (tests H1): megabase inference on 8 GB

H1 was the high-confidence pre-registration and it holds with a caveat worth stating: the k-mer table makes our arms less memory-light than pure KDA (D04 runs a 1 Mbp forward in 6.3 GB without offload; D06/D07 need the chunk-exact offload path above 512 K to keep the needle sweep alive). Still, on the metric that matters for consumer deployment — the longest single sequence processable at all — our arms reach 1 Mbp where HyenaDNA-1M reaches 32 K (OOM at 64 K) and Caduceus-PS 16 K (OOM at 32 K).

Longer inputs help on species identity (monotone to 256 K) but hurt on GLRB variant-effect prediction, where every model degrades from 8 K to 32 K windows (D06: 0.672→0.578) — diluting a SNP-centered signal with context is not free. "More context" is a per-task question, and the harness answers it per task rather than assuming the long-context story is uniformly positive.

The headline result of the project sits here. D06's worst downstream failure — splice sites, where its 0.618 fine-tuned accuracy fell below a randomly-initialized copy of itself (0.86) — was direct evidence that 131 K-only pretraining damages sub-kilobase positional grammar rather than merely not helping it. D07's window-plus-curriculum repairs exactly that class of task: splice donors 0.951, splice all 0.922, now well above the random-init control, plus consistent gains on enhancer/OCR/regulatory-element classification. But the repair is paid for on GUE's epigenetic-mark tasks, which are dominated by soft compositional signals over 500 bp; one plausible reading is that block 4's exact local recall displaces mixer capacity these tasks were using, and the curriculum's short-sequence gradient steps re-tune timescales that the emp_* tasks preferred long. Perplexity predicted none of this — the two arms are 0.002 nats apart (§6.1) and 5–30 points apart per task in both directions, this domain's restatement of "perplexity is not capability."

Table 4 — verdicts on the pre-registered hypotheses. "Supported" means the falsifier stated in §3 did not occur; H4's GUE regression was not predicted in either direction and is reported as a discovered trade-off, not a confirmation.

  • The composite is not yet decomposed. D07 changes two things over D06. The single-axis controls exist as configs (d06_curriculum.yaml: curriculum without window; D06 itself: neither) but the curriculum-only control was untrained at this draft's checkpoint; attributing the splice-site gain between window and curriculum — and the GUE loss likewise — is the single most important pending run.
  • ≈90% checkpoints, one seed. Numbers are from step ~34 K of 38,147, single seed, single data order. Loss curves are flat enough that the last 10% is unlikely to reorder arms, but final-checkpoint numbers will replace these; bootstrap CIs cover split-sampling uncertainty only.
  • Capacity ceiling. At dmodel=128, NT promoter_all is pinned at ~0.85–0.87 under every head, learning rate, and initialization tried (GC content alone yields 0.81) — a model-capacity ceiling, reported as such. The ~2.3M-parameter scale (chosen for training throughput on the 8 GB card, well below the 100M originally envisioned) bounds every accuracy claim.
  • Comparisons with released baselines are efficiency comparisons. HyenaDNA/Caduceus columns share our harness but not our pretraining budget; where they match or beat us the reading is unremarkable, where we match them at 2.3M parameters the reading is efficiency, and their OOM ceilings describe 8 GB deployment of those checkpoints, not the methods at their native scale.
  • What we would test next. The curriculum-only control; finishing the last 10% and regenerating all tables; a g/window-size sweep of the local layer; and state-utilization diagnostics (participation ratio of the recurrent state) to localize the 4 K→64 K recall decay inside slot fast weights.

8Conclusion

Gated delta-rule linear attention is a workable base operator for character-level genomic modeling on consumer hardware — but not alone. Pure KDA's finite-state recall bound is not a distant cliff on DNA; it is a wall at distance zero. A content-addressed k-mer fast-weight memory removes most of that wall for ~3% extra parameters while preserving O(N) scaling and megabase-length exact chunked inference on an 8 GB GPU, which the released convolutional and SSM checkpoints cannot match on the same card. And the fixed ultra-long pretraining context that makes such models impressive at length makes them worse at sub-kilobase grammar than their own random initialization — a failure a single local-attention layer and a length curriculum repair, at a measurable cost elsewhere. The trade-offs, not the wins, are the finding: recall, context length, and short-range grammar are three different budgets, and an architecture choice that spends one is spending it from somewhere.
